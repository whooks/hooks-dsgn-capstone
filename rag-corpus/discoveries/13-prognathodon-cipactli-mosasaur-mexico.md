# Prognathodon cipactli: a powerful-jawed sea reptile from Mexico

Summary: Scientists identified a new species of mosasaur — a marine reptile from the age of the dinosaurs — from a skull found in northern Mexico. The roughly six-meter-long predator had powerful jaws and was named Prognathodon cipactli.

## What was found

The new species, Prognathodon cipactli, is a mosasaur: a large, ocean-going reptile that lived during the Late Cretaceous, contemporary with the dinosaurs but not a dinosaur itself. The animal was about six meters long with robust, powerful jaws suited to crushing prey. It was defined from a skull discovered about 25 years earlier in northern Mexico and studied in detail more recently. The species name "cipactli" references a primordial sea creature from Aztec mythology.

## Why it matters

The find adds to the record of mosasaur diversity in the ancient seas that once covered parts of present-day Mexico, and highlights how museum and field specimens collected decades ago can yield new species when re-examined with modern study. Prognathodon was a genus of especially heavy-jawed mosasaurs thought to have fed on hard or large prey, so the new species helps illustrate the range of feeding strategies among these marine predators.

## Key terms

- Mosasaur: a large marine reptile of the Cretaceous seas (related to modern monitor lizards and snakes, not a dinosaur).
- Holotype: the single specimen used to formally define a new species.
