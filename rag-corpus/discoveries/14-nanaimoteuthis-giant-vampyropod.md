# Nanaimoteuthis haggarti: a giant octopus relative of the dinosaur-era seas

Summary: Researchers described Nanaimoteuthis haggarti, a roughly 19-meter-long vampyropod — a relative of modern octopuses and vampire squid — that was a top predator in the oceans during the age of the dinosaurs.

## What was found

Nanaimoteuthis haggarti is a vampyropod, the cephalopod group that today includes octopuses and the vampire squid. Reconstructed at around 19 meters long, it would have been an enormous soft-bodied predator in Cretaceous seas, feeding on fish, crustaceans, and other sea creatures. The fossil material comes from the Pacific coast region of Canada.

## Why it matters

Soft-bodied animals like octopuses and squid rarely fossilize well because they lack hard skeletons, so any fossil evidence of giant ancient cephalopods is remarkable. A vampyropod approaching 19 meters suggests that the dinosaur-era oceans hosted soft-bodied predators far larger than living octopuses, reshaping ideas about the size and ecological role these animals could reach. The find adds an unusual, non-vertebrate top predator to the familiar picture of mosasaurs and other marine reptiles ruling the Cretaceous seas.

## Key terms

- Cephalopod: the mollusc group including octopuses, squid, cuttlefish, and nautiluses.
- Vampyropod: the cephalopod lineage that includes octopuses and the vampire squid.
