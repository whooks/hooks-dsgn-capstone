# New giant dinosaur from Brazil with ties to a Spanish species

Summary: Brazilian scientists identified a new species of giant dinosaur whose closest relatives include a similar animal found in Spain — a link that reinforces how landmasses and their dinosaur faunas were connected earlier in the Mesozoic.

## What was found

Researchers in Brazil described a new species of large sauropod dinosaur from fossils unearthed in the country. Sauropods are the long-necked, long-tailed plant-eaters that include the largest land animals ever; many Cretaceous sauropods belong to the titanosaur group. The Brazilian species shows anatomical similarities to a comparable dinosaur previously found in Spain.

## Why it matters

A close relationship between a South American species and a European one supports the idea that these regions shared related dinosaur lineages, reflecting ancient land connections and dispersal routes before and during the breakup of the supercontinents. Such biogeographic links help paleontologists reconstruct how dinosaur groups spread across the globe and how continents were joined in deep time. The find adds to South America's already rich record of giant sauropods.

## Key terms

- Sauropod / titanosaur: long-necked plant-eating dinosaurs; titanosaurs are a large Cretaceous subgroup.
- Biogeography: the study of how living things are distributed geographically and why.
