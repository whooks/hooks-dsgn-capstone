# Spinosaurus mirabilis: a sabre-crested "hell heron" from the Sahara

Summary: Scientists named a new species of Spinosaurus, a large fish-eating dinosaur, from fossils found in Niger. Its standout feature is a tall, sword-shaped (scimitar) crest on its head. The find was published in the journal Science in February 2026.

## What was found

A team led by Professor Paul Sereno of the University of Chicago described Spinosaurus mirabilis from "astonishing" fossils recovered in the Sahara Desert of Niger. Spinosaurus is a spinosaurid — a group of semi-aquatic, fish-eating theropod (meat-eating) dinosaurs. The new species lived about 95 million years ago, during the Cretaceous Period, when the region was a network of rivers and wetlands rather than desert.

## Why it matters

The most distinctive feature is a large, curved, sword-shaped crest on top of the skull. In life this bony crest would have been covered in keratin (the material in fingernails and bird beaks), like the casque of a cassowary, making it even taller. Combined with the sail along its back, the crest probably served for display — attracting mates or intimidating rivals.

Sereno described the animal as a kind of "hell heron" that waded into shallow water on sturdy legs to hunt the large fish of the day, rather than fully diving after prey. The previously known species, Spinosaurus aegyptiacus, had a much smaller crest and lived in a different area, so the new find expands the known diversity and range of spinosaurs.

## Caveats

All known specimens of Spinosaurus mirabilis are not fully grown; researchers estimate it was roughly eight meters long when it died but could have grown larger. Spinosaur skeletons are often fragmentary, so many basic questions about these dinosaurs remain open.
