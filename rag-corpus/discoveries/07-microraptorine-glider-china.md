# 120-million-year-old gliding predator that hunted birds (NW China)

Summary: A roughly 120-million-year-old fossil from northwestern China is changing how scientists understand an unusual group of small, gliding, feathered predatory dinosaurs — and shows direct evidence that one hunted early birds.

## What was found

The fossil belongs to a microraptorine-type dinosaur — small, feathered, predatory theropods related to the famous four-winged Microraptor. These animals had long feathers on both their arms and legs, allowing them to glide between trees. The specimen dates to the Early Cretaceous, about 120 million years ago, a time when feathered dinosaurs and early birds shared the forests of what is now China.

## Why it matters

The find adds detail to how these gliding predators lived and what they ate, including evidence pointing to the hunting of birds. It reinforces the picture, built from many Chinese fossils, of a Cretaceous world where the line between "dinosaur" and "bird" was blurry: small feathered dinosaurs and true birds coexisted, and some dinosaurs were active aerial or gliding predators. Fossils like this help document the evolutionary transition from feathered dinosaurs to modern birds.

## Key terms

- Theropod: the group of mostly meat-eating dinosaurs that includes birds.
- Microraptorine: small, feathered, gliding dinosaurs with flight feathers on all four limbs.
