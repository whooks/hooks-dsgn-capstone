# Alnashetri: a tiny bird-like dinosaur from Patagonia

Summary: A new study of Alnashetri, a small, bird-like dinosaur from Argentina dated to about 90 million years ago, gives fresh insight into how this lineage of slender, lightly built theropods evolved, shrank in size, and spread.

## What was found

Alnashetri is a small, lightly built theropod (meat-eating dinosaur lineage) from the Late Cretaceous of Patagonia, southern Argentina. At roughly 90 million years old, the fossil helps fill in the record of slender, bird-like dinosaurs in the Southern Hemisphere. Researchers used the specimen to study how body size in this group changed over time and how these animals dispersed across ancient landmasses.

## Why it matters

Small-bodied, bird-like dinosaurs are important for understanding the evolutionary road toward modern birds, but their delicate bones fossilize less often than those of giants, so each well-preserved specimen is valuable. Alnashetri adds a Southern Hemisphere data point to a story often dominated by Northern Hemisphere fossils, helping paleontologists trace how these lineages evolved and shrank over the course of the Cretaceous.

## Key terms

- Theropod: the group of bipedal, mostly meat-eating dinosaurs that includes birds.
- Patagonia: a region of southern South America rich in Cretaceous dinosaur fossils.
