# Tylosaurus rex: a giant new mosasaur, the "T. rex of the sea"

Summary: Scientists identified a massive new mosasaur — a marine reptile that lived alongside the dinosaurs — named Tylosaurus rex. It measured up to 43 feet long and is among the largest mosasaurs ever found.

## What was found

The research was led by the American Museum of Natural History, the Perot Museum of Nature and Science (Dallas), and Southern Methodist University. Lead author Amelia Zietlow noticed that a fossil in a museum collection had been misidentified as the species Tylosaurus proriger. Comparing it with the original T. proriger fossil revealed it was a different animal. More than a dozen museum specimens were eventually reassigned to the new species, Tylosaurus rex.

Mosasaurs were large, ocean-going reptiles (not dinosaurs) that dominated the seas in the Cretaceous Period. T. rex (the mosasaur) lived about 80 million years ago in what is now Texas, reached up to 43 feet — roughly twice the length of the largest great white sharks — and had finely serrated teeth plus powerful jaw and neck muscles.

## Why it matters

Beyond its size, fossils show evidence of violent encounters between members of the same species: one specimen nicknamed "The Black Knight" is missing its snout tip and has a fractured jaw. The study also revised the main dataset used to map mosasaur evolution, which had barely changed in three decades, suggesting many earlier conclusions should be revisited. Famous fossils once labeled T. proriger — including "Bunker" and "Sophie" — are now reassigned to T. rex.

Note: This marine "T. rex" (Tylosaurus rex) is a different animal from the dinosaur Tyrannosaurus rex; they share the species name "rex" (Latin for "king") but are not closely related.
