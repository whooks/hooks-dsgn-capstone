# Nagatitan: the largest dinosaur found in Southeast Asia

Summary: Researchers identified a new species of giant long-necked dinosaur in Thailand — the largest ever found in Southeast Asia. Named Nagatitan, it has been estimated at roughly 90 feet long and as heavy as about nine elephants (around 27 tonnes).

## What was found

The discovery began with bones found at the edge of a pond in Chaiyaphum, Thailand, about a decade before the announcement. After years of study, scientists determined the remains belonged to a previously unknown species. Nagatitan is a sauropod — the group of plant-eating dinosaurs with very long necks and tails, which includes the largest land animals ever to have lived. More specifically it belongs among the titanosaurs, the giant sauropods that dominated the Cretaceous Period.

## Why it matters

At an estimated 90 feet (about 27 metres) long and a mass comparable to nine elephants, Nagatitan is the largest dinosaur yet identified in Southeast Asia. The find extends the known range of giant titanosaurs into the region and helps paleontologists understand how these enormous animals were distributed across the ancient world. The name references the Naga, a serpent figure in Southeast Asian mythology, combined with "titan."

## Key terms

- Sauropod: a long-necked, long-tailed, plant-eating dinosaur.
- Titanosaur: a group of especially large sauropods common in the Cretaceous.
