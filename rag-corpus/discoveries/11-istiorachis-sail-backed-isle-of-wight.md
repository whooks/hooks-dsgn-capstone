# Istiorachis macarthurae: a sail-backed dinosaur from the Isle of Wight

Summary: A new plant-eating dinosaur with a sail-like ridge along its back was identified on Britain's Isle of Wight and named Istiorachis macarthurae, honoring round-the-world sailor Dame Ellen MacArthur.

## What was found

Istiorachis macarthurae was described from fossils on the Isle of Wight, a location off the south coast of England famous for its Early Cretaceous dinosaur remains. It was an iguanodontian — a plant-eating ornithopod dinosaur — distinguished by tall spines along its vertebrae that would have supported a sail or ridge running down its back. The species name macarthurae honors the British sailor Dame Ellen MacArthur, a nod to the sail-like structure ("Istiorachis" refers to a sail-backed spine).

## Why it matters

The sail or tall-spined back may have been used for display (such as attracting mates or signaling to rivals), a recurring theme among dinosaurs that evolved elaborate crests, sails, and frills. The Isle of Wight continues to be one of Europe's richest sources of dinosaur fossils, and each new species helps document the diversity of Early Cretaceous ecosystems in what is now Britain.

## Key terms

- Iguanodontian: a group of plant-eating ornithopod dinosaurs.
- Neural spines: upward projections of the vertebrae that can support a sail or ridge.
