# Why did the (non-bird) dinosaurs go extinct?

Short answer: About 66 million years ago, a massive asteroid impact — likely combined with intense volcanic activity — triggered a sudden global catastrophe that ended the Cretaceous Period and wiped out the non-bird dinosaurs along with about three-quarters of all species. Birds are the surviving lineage of dinosaurs.

## The main cause: an asteroid impact

The leading explanation is the impact of an asteroid roughly 10 kilometers wide that struck near what is now Chicxulub, on Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, about 66 million years ago. Evidence includes a worldwide layer of sediment rich in iridium (an element rare on Earth's surface but common in asteroids), shocked quartz, glassy spherules, and the buried Chicxulub crater itself. This boundary marks the end of the Cretaceous Period and is called the K–Pg boundary (Cretaceous–Paleogene).

## Why the impact was so deadly

The impact threw enormous amounts of dust, soot, and vaporized rock into the atmosphere. This blocked sunlight for months to years, causing a sharp drop in temperatures (an "impact winter") and collapsing photosynthesis. Food chains failed from the bottom up: plants died back, then plant-eaters, then the predators that depended on them.

## The role of volcanism

Around the same time, immense volcanic eruptions in India known as the Deccan Traps were releasing gases that stressed the global climate. Many scientists think the eruptions weakened ecosystems before the impact delivered the final blow. The relative importance of impact versus volcanism is still debated, but the asteroid is regarded as the decisive trigger.

## What survived

The extinction was selective. Birds — which are themselves a branch of theropod dinosaurs — survived, as did mammals, crocodiles, turtles, amphibians, and many fish and insects. Because birds are dinosaurs, scientists are careful to say the "non-avian" (non-bird) dinosaurs went extinct, while dinosaurs in the broad sense live on as roughly 11,000 species of birds today.
