# How are fossils formed?

Short answer: Most fossils form when an organism is buried quickly by sediment, and over long periods its hard parts are gradually replaced or filled in by minerals, turning bone into rock. Fossilization is rare, which is why the fossil record is incomplete.

## The usual steps (permineralization)

1. Death and rapid burial. An animal dies and is covered by sediment — sand, mud, or volcanic ash — before scavengers, weather, or decay destroy it. Rapid burial, often in water-laid sediment, greatly improves the odds of preservation.
2. Decay of soft parts. Flesh and other soft tissues usually rot away, leaving hard parts such as bones, teeth, and shells.
3. Mineralization. Groundwater carrying dissolved minerals seeps into the porous bone. Over thousands to millions of years, minerals precipitate in the spaces and sometimes replace the original material, producing a rock-like fossil that preserves the bone's shape.
4. Burial and uplift. The fossil-bearing rock is buried deeper over time. Much later, geological uplift and erosion can expose the fossil at the surface, where it may be found.

## Other kinds of fossils

- Trace fossils record activity rather than the body itself: footprints, burrows, nests, eggs, and even fossilized dung (coprolites).
- Molds and casts form when an organism dissolves away, leaving a cavity (mold) that may later fill with minerals (cast).
- Exceptional preservation can capture soft tissues, skin, or feathers under special conditions such as fine-grained sediment, amber, tar, or ice.

## Why fossils are rare

Most organisms decompose or are scattered before burial; many fossils that do form are later destroyed by heat, pressure, or erosion. Hard-bodied animals in watery, sediment-rich environments fossilize most often, which biases the fossil record toward certain habitats and body types. As a result, the record is a partial sample of past life, and each new find can fill important gaps.

## Key terms

- Sediment: particles like sand, mud, or ash that settle and can bury remains.
- Permineralization: minerals filling the pore spaces of bone or wood.
- Trace fossil: preserved evidence of activity (tracks, burrows, eggs, dung).
