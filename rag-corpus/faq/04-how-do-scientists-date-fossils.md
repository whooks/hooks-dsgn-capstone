# How do scientists know how old a fossil is?

Short answer: Scientists combine relative dating (the order of rock layers) with absolute dating (radiometric methods that measure radioactive decay) to estimate a fossil's age. Dinosaur fossils are usually dated by dating the volcanic rock layers around them.

## Relative dating: reading the rock layers

Sedimentary rocks form in layers (strata), with older layers generally below younger ones — the principle of superposition. A fossil's position relative to other layers shows whether it is older or younger than nearby fossils. "Index fossils" — species known to have lived during a narrow time span — help match the ages of rock layers in different places. Relative dating gives order, but not a number in years.

## Absolute dating: radiometric methods

To assign ages in years, scientists use radiometric dating, which relies on radioactive isotopes that decay at a known, constant rate measured by their "half-life" (the time for half the atoms to decay). By measuring the ratio of a parent isotope to its decay product in a sample, they calculate how long decay has been occurring.

- Radiocarbon (carbon-14) dating works only on organic material up to roughly 50,000 years old — far too young for dinosaurs.
- For dinosaur-age rocks (millions of years), scientists use methods such as uranium–lead or potassium–argon (argon–argon) dating on minerals in volcanic ash and lava layers.

## Why volcanic layers matter

Most fossils form in sedimentary rock, which usually cannot be radiometrically dated directly. Instead, scientists date volcanic ash or lava layers above and below the fossil. If a fossil lies between a layer dated to 67 million years and one dated to 66 million years, the fossil falls within that range. This "bracketing" is how many dinosaur ages are established.

## Cross-checking

Researchers combine multiple lines of evidence — rock layering, radiometric dates, index fossils, and sometimes magnetic reversals recorded in the rock (paleomagnetism) — to produce reliable, cross-checked ages.

## Key terms

- Half-life: the time for half of a radioactive isotope to decay.
- Stratigraphy: the study of rock layers and their order.
- Index fossil: a fossil species used to date the rock layer it is found in.
